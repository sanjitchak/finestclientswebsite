00

The core principle behind everything

Before you touch a single ad, you need to understand who we are actually trying to reach.

Every business owner says the same thing: "I want more qualified leads." And almost everyone defines "qualified" the wrong way. They think a qualified lead is someone who has money.

That's a weak filter. Thanks to credit, almost everyone has access to money. But having money does not mean someone is willing to spend it. Often the people with the most money are the stingiest with it — they ask for discounts, compare everything, delay, overthink, and want free advice first.

Memorize This
The best customers are not the people who have money.
They're the people who already spend money on the problem we solve.

Someone who has paid for gyms, trainers and diet plans is a better fitness lead than a millionaire who never bought anything. Someone who has bought courses, coaching, or agency services before is a better lead for us. Buyers buy. The best predictor of a future buyer is a past buyer.

This one idea shapes everything downstream: the questions on our application forms, the angles in our ad copy, even which website visitors we tell Facebook to find more of. Whenever you're qualifying a lead, don't just ask "can this person afford it?" Ask:

"What have you already tried to solve this?"
"What have you already bought, used, or invested in?"
"Roughly how much have you spent so far?"
Why it works Past spending tells you three things at once: they genuinely feel the problem, they believe in paid solutions, and they're already actively trying to get the result. When people answer "I've spent ₹1,000+" on a form, we can even redirect them to a special page with a tracking pixel — so Facebook's algorithm learns to find more people like them.

01

Research — know the market before you spend a rupee

Great ads are 80% research. We steal proven angles from competitors and pull real pain language from real people.

Step 1.1Spy on ads that are already winning

Facebook publishes every active ad in the Meta Ads Library. Here's the logic: if an ad has been running for a long time and racking up impressions, the advertiser is almost certainly making money with it. We study those, not random ads.

  1. Search the Ads Library with broad keywords for the client's niche (use keyword_unordered search and active ads only, sorted by total impressions).
  2. Scroll manually. For each keyword, collect up to 10 unique landing page URLs — the actual funnels behind the ads.
  3. Only keep real coach/consultant funnels: VSL pages, webinars, challenges, applications, consultation bookings.
Keep
  • VSL, webinar & challenge pages
  • Application / booking funnels
  • One clean root URL per funnel
Throw out
  • Facebook lead forms & fb.me links
  • Instagram / WhatsApp / Linktree / social profiles
  • App-store links, e-commerce, clinics & medspas
  • Duplicate variants of the same funnel

If you only find 6 valid funnels, report 6 and say so. Never pad the list with junk — bad research poisons everything built on top of it.

Step 1.2Build the buyer persona (the deep dive)

Next, we build a full psychological profile of the buyer. We use an AI deep-dive prompt that acts as a world-class direct response copywriter and maps out:

  • Deepest pain points — and what they've already tried that failed.
  • The dream solution and their beliefs (true or false) about the market.
  • The dominant emotion of the market and the exact language that connects.
  • The "Without / Even if" formula: "You can get [big benefit] without [the thing they fear most], even if [their biggest limitation]."
The Forum Foraging Method

The gold is in verbatim quotes. We pull exact wording from Reddit, Quora, G2 reviews and Facebook groups, because people describe their pain better than any copywriter can invent it. We hunt for:

  • "Bleeding neck" problems — pain so urgent they need a solution now, not a nice-to-have.
  • What keeps them up at 2am, and the pressure from bosses, family, or competitors.
  • The cost of inaction — money lost, time wasted, deals going to faster competitors.
  • Failed guru advice they've tried, and their skepticism about solutions like ours.
  • The identity trigger that makes them stop scrolling and think "that's for me."
  • The transformation: before state → after state, with a concrete measurable outcome.

We also validate the market itself: who's running paid ads in this space, typical price points, and saturation on a 1–10 scale. The sweet spot is 5–6 — proven demand, but still room to differentiate. Finally the avatar gets a name and bio ("Noah from New York, Head of Sales, $160K/year"), with their deepest fear and dream outcome each summarized in one sentence.

Step 1.3If the client has data: mine the form responses

When a client has a CSV of survey or application answers, that's better than any guesswork. Upload it and run this prompt chain — and run the whole thing 2–3 times, because each pass surfaces different insights:

1. Understand this CSV file please
2. I am starting [PROGRAM], can you summarize this sheet for me
3. Create an ideal client profile based on the responses here
4. What psychographic insights did you get from the data
5. What negative things have you noticed in the response sheet
6. Based on the data, give me a curriculum
7. Analyze the feedback and give me repeating words or phrases,
   to be used in landing pages. Give the EXACT words.
Why step 7 matters most When your landing page uses the exact phrases your audience uses, readers feel like you're reading their mind. That's not magic — it's copy-paste from their own answers.

Step 1.4If there's no data: build the persona from scratch

No CSV? We use a structured persona prompt covering demographics, background, psychographics, professional and personal goals, media habits, purchasing behavior, technology usage, and pain points — woven into one coherent narrative person.

Then we run the Before/After exercise: acting as a market researcher who has talked to hundreds of people in the niche, answer these two questions 10 times each, going deeper each time:

  1. Summarize their current state (the painful present).
  2. Summarize their desired state (the dream outcome).

The gap between those two lists is the ad campaign.

Step 1.5Interest targeting: proxies for buyers

Remember Phase 00 — we want spenders. Since we can't target "people who spend money" directly, we target signals that correlate with it:

  • Life moments: birthdays (new beginnings), engaged credit-card users.
  • Devices: premium phone owners.
  • Locations: people near convention centers and five-star hotels (2 km radius).
  • Affluent hobbies: yoga, tennis, health clubs, spas, horse riding.
What you'll actually do in Phase 1
  • Collect 10 clean competitor funnel URLs per keyword from the Meta Ads Library.
  • Run the persona deep-dive with forum foraging; save verbatim quotes in a swipe doc.
  • If a CSV exists, run the 7-prompt chain 2–3× and extract exact repeated phrases.
  • Deliver: one avatar document + one "exact language" bank. Everything else builds on these.
02

Ad copy — words that make strangers care

Research gave us the ammunition. Now we write ads that pre-frame the reader before they ever reach the landing page.

The metric that exposes bad copyCTR vs CTR (All)

Diagnostic Rule
If CTR (All) and CTR are nearly the same, people are clicking without reading.

They arrive on the sales page with no context and no orientation — basically lost — and bounce. Good ad copy does half the selling before the click. A healthy ad has plenty of engagement (reads, expands, reactions) around its link clicks, which shows people consumed the message first.

FrameworkThe "What Happens Next" ad

This is our go-to long-copy framework. The psychology: people are more motivated to avoid loss than to gain something new — but you still need to move them from problem to solution. So you become a fortune teller and show them two futures:

  1. Call out symptoms they're already feeling right now.
  2. Predict the dark future — "what happens next" if they don't fix it. Go deeper into the problem state.
  3. Flip the script — "what happens next" once they DO take action, using the client's mechanism.
  4. Stack the timeline of wins — within days… within weeks… within months…
  5. Prove it — "these are the exact results over [X] people achieved with [the method]."
The engine of the framework The contrast between the two futures is what creates desire. Neither future alone sells — the gap between them does.

When writing it, don't lean on logic or feature lists. Tap into strong emotional desires: feeling safer, smarter, superior, or seen. And always write it USING the buyer persona from Phase 1 — never from imagination.

UnlearningNine copywriting "rules" that are actually half-truths

You'll hear these rules everywhere. Each has a kernel of truth and a bigger truth behind it:

The "rule"The actual truth
"Keep sentences short."Rhythm converts, not length. Mix short punches with longer flowing lines — monotony is the enemy.
"Never repeat yourself."Repetition with variation is hypnotic. Each restatement of the promise answers a different doubt.
"Clear, never clever."Clever anchored in clarity sticks. "Stop coaching like a side hustle. Start running it like a real business." Vagueness is the enemy, not wit.
"One CTA only."Multiple CTAs work when they meet different readiness levels: "Join now" for the ready, "Grab the free guide" for the curious.
"Never say 'I'."Strategic vulnerability sells. Your story isn't the hero — it's the mirror. "I felt this" makes them think "me too."
"No emotion in B2B."Business buyers are still humans afraid of making the wrong call. Naming what they really feel is honest, not unprofessional.
"People don't read long copy."People don't read boring copy. A 2,000-word piece that feels written just for them gets read to the end.
"Follow the formula (PAS, AIDA)."Formulas are training wheels. Once you know why they work, you can lead with the dream instead of the pain and still convert.
"Qualified = has money."You know this one already. Qualified = spends money. Weave the spending-behavior angle into the copy itself.

Headline machineFormulas + the refinement chain

Headlines are fill-in-the-blank structures fed with your persona DATA. A few of the workhorses:

  • The Lazy [Avatar]'s Way to [Primary Goal]
  • How to [Achieve Desired Outcome] in [Specific Time Frame]
  • [Number] Mistakes Most People Make When [Common Action]
  • You Don't Have to Be [Something Challenging] to Be [Desired Result]
  • Why You're [Not Achieving Outcome] — and How to Fix It
  • Get Rid of [Problem] Once and For All

Then we run a refinement chain — each prompt improves the last output:

1. Act as a direct response copywriter who uses visceral, real-life,
   simple but dimensional language. Create 20 headlines using
   FORMULA structures + DATA. Some as questions. No colons,
   no exclamation points.
2. Rewrite each with dimensional, vivid examples in context.
3. Work the dimensional copy INTO the headline. Make each one
   mysterious enough that I have to keep reading.
4. Remove colons, commas, dashes. One complete thought.
5. You're writing too lofty. Keep it simple.

One more guardrail: we ban the classic AI-sounding words — empower, unleash, unlock, elevate, delve, tapestry, transform, journey, groundbreaking, discover — and ask instead: "Make me see it."

Sales bulletsFascination bullets that create intrigue

Bullets tease, they never reveal. The pattern: imply a specific, valuable benefit while withholding the answer — "The #1 thing you must never do…", "What [experts] know about [topic] that you don't…", "How to X without Y…"

Run the bullet prompt 2–3 times against the client DATA, mixing the formula order (never top-to-bottom). Then refine: swap numbers for •, make them more aggressive or softer as the brand needs, remove the company name to add mystery, and always end the stack with "• And much, much more!"

Final passHumanize + compliance

Remove titles from headlines and remove all em dashes.
Humanize the language and bring it down to a fifth-grade reading level.
Open with and use emojis where the platform suits it (FB feed posts).
Keep each primary text in its own block so it can be copied in the same format.
Add the landing page link at the end.
Health/medical niches: no medical claims, no cures, no guarantees. Replace risky words with compliant phrasing. Supportive, non-judgmental tone.
What you'll actually do in Phase 2
  • Draft one "What Happens Next" long ad from the persona doc.
  • Generate 20 headlines through the 5-step refinement chain; shortlist 5.
  • Write 10 fascination bullets (2–3 passes), end with "And much, much more!"
  • Run the humanize + compliance checklist before anything ships.
03

Creative — the images and videos people actually see

Copy carries the argument; creative earns the attention. We produce in volume, with strict quality rules.

Image adsGenerating ad images with AI

We generate square ad images from detailed JSON prompts (usually 6 at a time, each in its own text block so they're easy to copy). Two rules govern every image concept:

  1. Bake in the qualification angle. Images should speak to people who already spend on the problem — "buyers buy" applies to visuals too.
  2. Feed the generator specifics. Use the fill-in-the-blank chain: What kind of images would promote [product]? → List the adjectives of that scene → Describe the scene in detail → paste all of it into the image generator.

For fresh angles, we also ask open-ended questions: how to visually represent the product's benefits, how to use color psychology for this audience, what visual metaphor makes the ad memorable, and how to work user-generated content or social proof into the visual.

Visual formatsMake it look native, not like an ad

One useful note from the Sanjit process doc: the first job of an ad image is to blend into the kind of content people already stop for. Build a swipe file around formats like Breaking News, Raw Native, SMS Screenshot, and visual pattern interrupt. Then test one visual style with several headline variations before moving on.

Video editing rulesThe Ad Video Codex

Every ad video gets edited with subtitle-based motion graphics: read the video's transcript, understand what's being said at each moment, and add a relevant visual graphic for that exact moment. Non-negotiables:

Always
  • Graphics relevant to the spoken line — never random decoration
  • Keep graphics compact, clean, colorful, animated
  • Place graphics above the speaker's head area
  • Vary images, icons and colors across moments
  • Export vertical and a 1000×1000 square version
Never
  • Cover the face, mouth, eyes, or subtitles
  • Put text inside the motion graphics
  • Reuse the same image across videos or timelines
  • Leave internal labels in the export ("VSL 01", "1 min")

The Mechanism, in one sentence

A mechanism is the simultaneous explanation of why everything they tried before failed AND why this will be different. The script shape:

Most people trying to get [result] do [X] → here's the problem with that → which ultimately means [consequence] → so instead, we do [our method] → which allows us to [benefit] → which ultimately means [benefit of the benefit].

The more sophisticated and competitive the market, the more you should focus on ONE key mechanism instead of a list of reasons.

ShortcutReverse-engineering YouTube video ads

  1. Right-click a YouTube ad → Copy debug info.
  2. Paste it into an AI chat and ask for the two YouTube links inside — the ad URL and the actual video URL.
  3. Ask: "Analyze this video ad's transcription for hook, story and CTA — and why is this ad working?"
  4. Estimate spend: take the view count and date, look up current YouTube CPM rates for the region, and calculate roughly what they've spent.
  5. Finish with: "For you to adapt this ad for my business, what information do you need?" — and let the AI interview you.

Landing pages — where clicks become leads

The ad wins attention; the page wins the conversion. Two page types, one big mindset shift.

The mindset shiftAwareness is a journey, not a badge

Beginners think a reader arrives wearing a badge — "problem-aware", "solution-aware", "ready to buy" — and that you need a different page for each. Wrong frame. A sales page isn't written FOR an awareness level. It's written in an ORDER that creates awareness. One page, right sequence, works for everyone:

  1. Start with the problem — even for people who already know it. The problem is neutral ground: the beginner feels seen, the researcher feels validated, the comparison-shopper feels reassured they're in the right place. Everyone keeps reading.
  2. Show WHY the problem exists. Most pages skip this. After recognizing the problem, the reader's next thought isn't "what should I buy?" — it's "why does this keep happening to me?" Answer that first.
  3. Show why the obvious solutions don't work. If you don't address "can't I just Google it / watch YouTube?", they'll leave and try that first. Knowing concepts and having a system are different things.
  4. Only now, present the product. All three reader types have arrived at the same realization: "I need a real solution, not more information."
Most sales pages don't fail because of bad copy. They fail because steps are skipped — the writer gets bored with the problem before the reader does.

Page type AThe opt-in page — 5 essential elements

One job: get contact details in exchange for a high-value content offer. Even though it's free, the prospect pays with their data — so you're still selling the opt-in.

Headline — grabs them by the throat with a specific, vivid benefit. Formula: Finally! How to Get [Result] Without [Thing They Fear Most] in [Time Frame].
Subheadline — restates exactly what they get and what to do ("Enter your email below for instant access").
Fascination bullets — the intrigue-cranking teasers from Phase 2. Imply the benefit, never give away the answer.
Visual mockup — show, don't tell. A high-quality mockup of the guide/checklist/course so they can "see" the value.
The form — name and email ONLY. Every extra field lowers conversion.

Page type BThe sales page — the 17-step selling system

For long-form sales pages, VSL pages and webinars (from Sell Like Crazy). The order matters — it's a persuasion staircase:

Call out your audience — qualify instantly ("Attention: Agency Owners").
Demand attention with a Big Promise headline.
Back up the promise in the subheadline.
Create intrigue with fascination bullets on pains, fears, hopes.
Floodlight the problem — describe their struggle better than they can.
Provide the solution.
Show credentials — case studies, awards, numbers.
Detail the benefits — features tell, benefits sell.
Social proof — third-party validation.
Make the Godfather Offer — see below.
Add bonuses — desirable sweeteners, not essentials.
Stack the value — list everything with a total monetary value.
Reveal the price — small against the stacked value.
Inject scarcity — genuine reasons to act now.
Power guarantee — reverse the risk completely.
Call to action — a specific command, not a request.
Close with a P.S. — warning + offer reminder + scarcity.
The Godfather Offer checklist

Copy is useless if the offer is weak. The offer must be so good "only a lunatic would refuse." Every Godfather Offer includes:

  • Rationale — why are you being this generous? (Unexplained generosity feels like a trap.)
  • Value build — establish the "regular" price so the deal is visible.
  • Payment options — plans lower the barrier to entry.
  • Premiums — free gifts/bonuses included.
  • Power guarantee — beyond "satisfaction guaranteed." Be specific: "results in 90 days or we work for free."

High-ticket servicesSell the call, not the product

For consulting and high-ticket services (that's us and most of our clients), don't sell from the page — sell a strategy call. After they accept, make them fill an application/survey. The psychology: jumping through hoops flips the power dynamic — they're chasing you. And the application questions? Phase 00: what have you tried, what have you bought, how much have you spent.

Universal rulesPrinciples for every page

Temperature matching: cold traffic (ads) → opt-in page first. Don't propose marriage on the first date. Hot traffic can go straight to a sales page.
The "fold" is a myth. People DO read long-form copy — if it's entertaining. Long-form beats short-form for sales conversions.
80 cents of your dollar is spent on the headline. If the headline fails, the page fails.
Know your unit economics. You must know CPL and CPA to know if a page is profitably performing.
Writing style: short sentences, simple words, no em dashes, strong direct statements, no vague claims, no fake numbers or invented testimonials.
"If you can't pay money to acquire a new customer, you do not have a business."Sabri Suby — Sell Like Crazy
What you'll actually do in Phase 4
  • Audit any page against the 5 opt-in elements or the 17-step staircase — flag skipped steps.
  • Check the reader sequence: problem → why it exists → why DIY fails → product.
  • Verify the offer passes the Godfather checklist before the page goes live.
  • Confirm application forms ask the three spending questions from Phase 00.
05

Launch & scale — multiply what's working

When an ad wins, we don't celebrate — we clone it, follow up relentlessly, and tighten the definition of a good lead.

Scaling a winnerChange the hook, keep the body

Once testing crowns a winning script, we scale it without breaking it. The golden rule: the body stays within ±10% of the winner's word count and structure — only the hook changes. The full scaling package:

Part A — 12 script variants. Same body length and flow as the winner; a new hook angle each time: emotion, curiosity, myth-bust, mistake→fix, story, authority. Spread the required themes across the 12 (e.g., at least 4 hooks per core topic).
Part B — image ad text. 3 formats (1080×1080 square, 1080×1920 story, 1200×1500 feed) × 3 angles (Authority, Relief/Emotion, How-To). Each gets a 5–7 word headline, ≤16-word subhead, 3 benefit bullets, price line, 2–3 word button, credibility micro-line — plus Meta primary text (125–150 chars), headline (≤30 chars), and alt text.
Part C — a 50-hook bank (8–12 words each) for daily creative volume. Mix emotions, curiosity, myth-busting, before→after. No claims.
Part D — compliance pass. Trim anything over winner length +10%, swap risky words (guarantee/cure/promise), confirm required mentions appear, return a QC checklist of edits made.
Why hooks, not bodies? The body is the proven sales argument — touching it risks the win. The hook is just the doorway, and different people walk through different doors. Twelve doorways, one proven room.

Follow-upEmail sequences by "extrapolation"

Most leads don't buy on day one, so email does the patient selling. Our rule is strict: every subject line and email angle must be pulled from something that already exists on the landing page. No random newsletters, no off-topic stories, no "just checking in."

  1. Extract from the page: headline + subhead, big promise/mechanism, benefit bullets, proof, demo, steps, mistakes to avoid, FAQ objections, guarantees, bonuses.
  2. Turn each extracted item into ONE email theme. Each email feels like a continuation of the page.
  3. Write two sequences: a 10-email non-buyer follow-up (opted in, didn't buy → drive back to the front-end page, a different page-derived theme every email), and a 3–7 email upsell sequence (bought front-end, didn't buy the upsell → themes only from the upsell page).

Format for every email: subject derived from page wording, optional preheader, 120–250 word body in simple language and short paragraphs, exactly one clear CTA matching the goal.

Lead qualityMeasure it, don't vibe it

"Lead quality" is too vague to improve. We pick 3–5 specific, measurable parameters from the client's paid-student survey (occupation, income, education, past course purchases, and so on) and track those. Separately, we survey paying customers for buying-intent signals — premium gadget ownership, homeownership, vehicle type, club memberships, travel frequency, luxury hobbies — and use those patterns to sharpen targeting.

And when we find a winning audience? Stop fiddling with the audience. Start focusing on messaging.

What you'll actually do in Phase 5
  • Build the 12-variant + 9-image-text + 50-hook scaling package for each winner.
  • Run the Part D compliance pass and attach the QC checklist.
  • Draft the 10-email non-buyer sequence, every theme traceable to the page.
  • Report lead quality against the 3–5 agreed parameters — numbers, not adjectives.

The refresh rhythm — because every ad dies

The most common way accounts blow up: a winner fatigues and there's nothing ready to replace it.

Here's the pattern that kills accounts: a campaign crushes for two weeks. Then week three hits — CPAs double, volume drops, and you're scrambling to make new creative under pressure. We've seen a large spender ride the same creatives for months, and when they finally fatigued it took six full weeks of poor performance to find viable replacements. The lesson:

The Non-Negotiable
Don't wait for ads to die. Refresh them before they do.

Whether the account runs complex manual bidding or simple broad targeting — both can work. The common thread in every account that scales is consistent creative refresh. That part is not optional.

ExperimentThe creative-variety test (Andromeda)

The old way: separate everything — images in one ad set, videos in another, square here, vertical there. The new approach we're testing: throw variety into the SAME ad set — reels, square videos, square images, vertical images, 4–5 different pictures — and let Facebook figure it out. The algorithm optimizes for creative variety; giving it options in one place lets it test formats against people faster.

Honest caveat: still under testing, not confirmed on every account. Where we've tried it, it's working — but treat it as an experiment, not a rule.

HousekeepingComment moderation

Active ads attract trolls, and negative comments tank ad performance. Every client page gets our keyword moderation list loaded into Facebook's page settings so troll comments are auto-hidden before they poison the social proof.

What you'll actually do in Phase 6
  • Own the Mon / Wed / Fri rhythm on assigned accounts — it keeps us ahead of fatigue instead of reacting to it.
  • Prepare light-refresh variants (color/layout/testimonial swaps) every 5–7 days.
  • Join the monthly 2-hour batch day; leave with 20–30 fresh creatives stockpiled.
  • Confirm the keyword moderation list is installed on every new client page.

Introduction to Facebook ads.

The mental model before any tactics: what Facebook ads really are, why we pick them over Google, and the hidden auction that decides who gets seen. You don't need any ads experience to follow this.

MODULE 01 WHY FB · DEMAND GEN · THE AUCTION BEGINNER FRIENDLY
1

The big picture: almost everyone is already here

The audience is huge, it spans four platforms, and it is visual. That combination is the whole reason we start on Facebook.

The audience is massive and it isn't going anywhere. As of early 2023, when this course was recorded, Facebook had roughly 2.9 billion monthly active users worldwide, including about 490 million in India, close to a third of the country in one place. Treat the exact figures as a dated snapshot rather than gospel, but the point holds: this is where the market lives.

And Facebook is no longer just Facebook. Meta owns four connected platforms, and one ad account reaches all of them:

  • Facebook: still the widest reach, especially for older Indian audiences.
  • Instagram: the second most used, strong for visual and younger buyers.
  • Messenger & WhatsApp: where you can drive people straight into a conversation.

There is one edge Google search can't match: Facebook is a visual medium. You tell a story with images, video, carousels, and collections. A search result is plain text. Here you grab attention with something people actually want to look at, and attention is the whole game.

2

Demand generation vs demand fulfilment

The single most important idea in this module. Get it and everything downstream clicks into place.

Here is the distinction that separates people who understand paid ads from people who just push buttons.

Memorize This
Google fulfils demand that already exists.
Facebook generates demand that doesn't.

On Google, people are searching. They already want the thing, so you just show up to meet them. Intent is high, but the market is capped at how many people are looking. On Facebook, nobody asked for your ad. You interrupt the scroll, so it has to be relevant or it gets ignored. In return, you reach people who didn't even know your solution existed. You're not fighting over existing demand. You're creating new demand.

A real example from the trainer: he bought a bamboo fibre towel from an Instagram ad. He'd never have searched Google for that, because he didn't know better towels existed. That's demand generation. Facebook planted the idea. Google could only have helped if he already wanted it.

3

You control the spend, and you can see everything

Two reasons Facebook is safe to learn on: hard budget caps, and metrics far deeper than Google gives you.

Cost control first. You set a daily budget (spend ₹1,000 a day) or a lifetime budget (₹50,000 across the whole campaign), and Facebook never overspends it. You decide the ceiling, not the platform.

Then measurability. The dashboard shows far more than Google ever exposes:

Reach and impressions (how many people, how many times shown)
Frequency (how often one person sees the same ad)
CTR (who actually clicked)
Landing page views (who arrived on the page)
Add-to-cart and checkout (who moved toward buying)
Purchases (who actually paid)
Why this matters on day one Because you can see every step, you can find the exact place people drop off and fix that one thing. Vague "the ads aren't working" becomes "80% reach the page but only 5% book". That is a fixable problem, not a mystery.
4

How the auction actually decides who gets seen

Every ad slot is an auction, but the highest bid does not automatically win. Relevance does most of the work.

This surprises almost everyone. You can bid less than a competitor and still win the slot. Here is the real formula:

The winning formula

Winner = your bid × your action rate. Action rate is basically how relevant and engaging your ad is. Facebook scores it with three rankings you'll see in the dashboard: quality ranking, engagement ranking (likes, comments, shares), and conversion rate ranking (leads and sales).

Why relevance beats a bigger bid Say you bid ₹1 and I bid ₹2 for the same audience. You'd expect me to win. But if your ad is more relevant and pulls 100 clicks while mine pulls 10, Facebook earns far more from you per impression, so it shows your cheaper ad more often. Relevant ads are cheaper and get shown more. This is exactly why we obsess over research and creative, not bid size.
5

Facebook vs Google, honestly

Real strengths, and the real costs you sign up for. Know both before you spend.
Strengths
  • Huge reach with precise targeting (job title, age, location, interests)
  • Visual storytelling that lifts engagement and conversion
  • Social proof: likes, comments, and shares make ads more persuasive
  • Usually lower cost per click than Google in the same niche
Watch-outs
  • Ad fatigue is fast. People go blind to the same ad, and some get annoyed
  • Search intent is low or zero. You're interrupting, not being invited
  • Results are less consistent, and privacy changes keep shrinking the data
  • Each ad points to one action, and it needs a team feeding it fresh creative

Google is closer to "set it and forget it": a search ad for a stable keyword can run for years untouched. Facebook is a living system that needs constant creative. Neither is better in the abstract. They do different jobs, and we use Facebook because we want to create demand, not just collect it.

6

The pro move: don't sell, warm the market

The mindset that turns cold strangers into buyers who did not exist yet.

The biggest beginner mistake is trying to sell the product directly in the ad. On a demand-generation platform, that's shoving a cold stranger toward something they never asked for. The trainer's approach, and ours, is different:

  1. Advertise free, valuable content, not the paid offer.
  2. Pull people into a funnel and capture their contact details.
  3. Nurture with email until trust is built.
  4. Then make the offer they can't resist.

This does something powerful. When your ads convince people they need a solution, you warm up the entire market, not just your own audience. Some of them take a year of emails before they buy. You're creating buyers who didn't exist yet.

The Mindset That Scales
Master the fundamentals, not the tricks.

Tactics are short-lived. Fundamentals let you invent your own tactics and stay ahead of the curve. When you understand the process, a campaign isn't emotional. You either know it will win, or you know exactly why it lost.

Why lead costs keep rising, and why that's normal Facebook costs climb over time because more advertisers compete for the same feed. A cosmetics brand isn't your competitor, but its ads still fill the space where yours could show. You can't optimize your way out of the market price forever. The real fix isn't endless ad tweaking. It's a strong offer, good nurture, and a sales process that earns enough per lead that you can happily pay the going rate and keep reinvesting.
What this means for you
  • Never judge an ad by bid size. Judge it by relevance: quality, engagement, and conversion rankings.
  • Think demand generation. We advertise content and warm people up; we don't hard-sell cold traffic.
  • Learn the fundamentals before chasing any tactic you see online.
  • Expect lead costs to drift up over time. The answer is a better funnel, not just a lower bid.

Before campaigns, there is infrastructure: Business Manager, ad accounts, pages, pixels, domains, payment methods, people, partners, and backups. This module is the map of who owns what.

MODULE 02 BUSINESS MANAGER · ACCESS · SECURITY OPS FOUNDATION
1

Why Business Manager matters

A personal ad account is fragile. A verified business setup gives the account a real legal identity.

The first principle: Meta trusts a real person first, then a real business. Do not create a fake profile just to create Business Manager. Use a complete, legitimate Facebook profile because everything still starts from that identity.

A Business Manager account gives you structure and recovery paths. You can verify the business with documents, add more people safely, request or share client access, centralize payments, and get better support if an ad account gets disabled.

Agency mindset Never ask a client for their personal Facebook login. Both sides should use Business Manager, then share only the assets required for the work.
2

The hierarchy: business first, assets below

Most confusion disappears when you stop thinking of it as one account and start seeing it as a container of assets.

The simple hierarchy is: Business Manager → ad accounts → pages and data sources. A page is the public identity that runs the ad. An ad account is where spend and campaigns live. A pixel is the data source that tracks what happens on the website.

3

Give access without giving away control

Business Manager exists so the right person can touch the right asset and nothing else.
Do
  • Add team members under People and assign only the assets they need.
  • Use Partners when an agency or client shares a business ID.
  • Give accountant-style access when someone only needs spend or invoice visibility.
  • Request ad account access instead of asking clients to transfer ownership.
Avoid
  • Sharing personal Facebook passwords.
  • Giving full Business Manager admin access to every employee.
  • Assuming pixel, domain, audience, and ad account access come together.
  • Making major access changes without checking who owns the asset.

For client work, the flow is usually: client owns the business and ad account, agency requests partner access, then the agency assigns its internal team members from its own Business Manager.

4

Pages, pixels, domains, audiences, and WhatsApp

Running ads is not just an ad account. The campaign needs all supporting assets connected.
PageThe identity shown on the ad. It can also publish organic posts.
InstagramLinked so ads can run under the correct IG identity.
WhatsAppCan connect to a business number, usually handled through tools like Interakt or similar providers.
PixelWebsite behavior data. It must be shared separately from the ad account.
DomainVerified with a meta tag so Meta knows you own the traffic destination.
AudienceCustomer lists, saved audiences, or website visitors. These can be shared without exposing raw customer data.
Important Distinction
Sharing an ad account does not automatically share the pixel, domain, or audience.

If a partner needs to optimize campaigns properly, check every required asset: ad account, page, pixel, domain, custom audiences, Instagram account, and payment access where relevant.

5

Safety setup before spend

Good operators reduce avoidable risk before the first campaign goes live.
Verify the business with proper documents where available.
Add a backup admin so one locked profile does not lock the company out.
Turn on two-factor authentication for profiles and Business Manager.
Add payment methods centrally when multiple owned ad accounts need billing access.
Verify domains before sending paid traffic.
Know where support lives, but do not depend on support to rescue policy mistakes.
Module 02 operating checklist
  • Create or confirm the Business Manager from a real, complete Facebook profile.
  • Map every asset: ad account, page, pixel, domain, Instagram, WhatsApp, audiences, payments.
  • Assign people and partners using least-privilege access.
  • Verify business, domain, two-factor authentication, and backup admin before launch.

Pixel, events, and conversion memory.

The pixel tells Meta what happens after the click. Without it, campaigns optimize for cheap clicks. With it, campaigns can learn who becomes a lead, who buys, and who should see the next ad.

MODULE 03 PIXEL · STANDARD EVENTS · CUSTOM CONVERSIONS TRACKING FOUNDATION
1

The pixel is a tiny code with a big job

It records page views and conversion behavior so Meta can optimize for people who take real actions.

The Meta pixel is a small JavaScript snippet tied to a unique pixel ID. By default it tracks PageView, meaning Meta can see that a visitor reached a page where the pixel loaded.

The old "pixel" name comes from the fallback tracking image: a hidden 1×1 image that could fire even when JavaScript was limited. Modern pixel setup is more powerful, but the idea is the same: send behavior data back to the ad account.

Why conversion data matters If you optimize for clicks, Meta finds clickers. If you optimize for leads or purchases, Meta studies the people who converted and tries to find more like them.
2

Install it site-wide, then prove it fires

Do not trust that a pixel exists until you have tested it from the browser and inside Events Manager.
  1. Create the pixel from Events Manager under the correct ad account.
  2. Install it manually or through a site-wide header/footer tool. On WordPress, the transcript uses Header Footer Code Manager.
  3. Enable advanced matching when appropriate so Meta can match more visitors to profiles.
  4. Open the page source and search for fbq, then use Meta Pixel Helper and Events Manager's test events.
3

Standard events tell Meta what action happened

Page views are only the start. Lead and Purchase events are what make optimization useful.
LeadUsually fires on the thank-you page after someone submits a form.
CompleteRegistrationUseful when a signup is more like account or webinar registration.
InitiateCheckoutShows that a buyer started the checkout flow.
PurchaseBest for sales campaigns, especially when value and currency are passed back.

Standard events can be installed as code or configured with the Event Setup Tool by tracking a URL or button. For lead generation, the simplest reliable pattern is: form submit → redirect to thank-you page → fire the Lead event there.

Optimization Rule
Do not run lead campaigns if the Lead event is not firing.

Otherwise Meta cannot learn who became a lead. It can only optimize for cheaper clicks or page views, which is a different population of people.

4

Standard events vs custom conversions

Both can measure outcomes, but they behave differently.
Standard eventCustom conversion
How it worksEvent code or event setup fires a named action like Lead or Purchase.Meta matches a URL rule, often a specific thank-you page.
Best forReusable events, purchases, dynamic product/cart values, custom audiences.Separating one funnel from another when both use the same event.
Value trackingCan pass dynamic value and currency for purchase/ROAS reporting.Usually fixed value, not dynamic.
AudiencesCan be used to build custom audiences and lookalikes.More limited; useful as a campaign optimization target.

Example: a lead magnet and a webinar may both fire Lead. If you want to optimize only for webinar registrations, create a custom conversion for the webinar thank-you page.

5

Pixels become audiences

Once events fire, you can retarget converters or ask Meta to find more people like them.

After enough people visit pages or trigger events, you can build custom audiences: all website visitors, people who fired Lead in the last 30 or 90 days, people who viewed checkout, buyers, or people who visited a specific page.

Those custom audiences become seeds for lookalike audiences. The cleaner the seed, the better the lookalike. A list of engaged leads or buyers is better than a raw list of everyone who ever joined an email list.

Module 03 setup checklist
  • Create the pixel under the correct ad account and install it site-wide.
  • Test PageView, then configure Lead and Purchase where relevant.
  • Use custom conversions for specific thank-you pages or funnel-specific outcomes.
  • Create custom audiences from important events, then use them for retargeting and lookalikes.

Campaign types, structure, and budget logic.

The campaign objective tells Meta what kind of person to find. Pick the wrong objective and the system can deliver exactly what you asked for, while your business gets nothing useful.

MODULE 04 OBJECTIVES · STRUCTURE · BUDGETING CAMPAIGN PLANNING
1

Every objective trains Meta for a different behavior

Clicks, views, leads, purchases, messages, and engagement are different audiences inside the same platform.
ObjectiveUse it whenBeginner warning
Awareness / reachYou want memory, visibility, or brand recall.Useful for brand building, not direct lead ROI.
Video viewsYou want people to watch 15 seconds or more, or finish short videos.Great for warming audiences, not necessarily leads.
TrafficYou want clicks or landing page views.Clickers are not always converters.
EngagementYou want likes, comments, shares, or social proof on a post.Comments can help or hurt if moderation is weak.

2

Do not ask a traffic campaign for leads

If you choose traffic, Meta finds people likely to click. If you choose leads, it finds people likely to convert.
Beginner Trap
A cheap click is not a lead.
A cheap lead is not a buyer.

The objective sets the learning target. A traffic campaign can get 200 clicks and zero leads because it did its job: it found clickers. For lead generation, use the Leads objective with a website conversion event whenever quality matters.

Instant forms reduce friction because Facebook pre-fills name and email. That can lower CPL, but quality often drops because people submit casually or forget they opted in. A landing page form adds friction, but the lead usually understands the offer better.

3

The starting structure: one campaign, multiple tests

Give Meta enough variation to learn, but keep the structure simple enough to read.

The recommended beginner structure is one campaign, three to four ad sets, and four ads per ad set. Each ad set gets one broad interest so you can see which audience is actually producing results.

4

Budgeting starts from funnel math, not guessing

You cannot answer "how much should I spend?" without conversion assumptions.

The budgeting sheet uses the funnel backwards. Decide how many leads or sales you need, then estimate the inputs:

Landing page conversion rate — out of 100 visitors, how many fill the form or buy?
Link click to landing page ratio — out of 100 clicks, how many actually load the page?
CTR — out of 100 impressions, how many click the ad?
CPM — cost to buy 1,000 impressions in that audience.
Target volume — how many leads or sales you need in the campaign period.
Use real numbers quickly The first sheet uses assumptions. After 10,000 impressions or a few days of data, replace assumptions with your actual CPM, CTR, landing page views, and conversion rate.
5

Monitor because Facebook is not set-and-forget

Costs move as Meta reaches colder people and frequency rises.

Facebook usually shows ads first to the people most likely to convert. As you spend more, it must reach colder people, CTR can drop, frequency can rise, and CPL can climb. That is why every campaign needs daily or at least weekly monitoring.

Module 04 launch checklist
  • Use Leads or Sales when the business needs leads or sales.
  • Start with one campaign, three to four ad sets, and four ads per ad set.
  • Put each broad interest in its own ad set so results are readable.
  • Calculate budget from CPM, CTR, link-click-to-LP ratio, and landing page conversion.
  • Refresh assumptions with real data after three to seven days or around 10,000 impressions.

MODULE 05 LEADS · TARGETING · AUDIENCES HANDS-ON BUILD
1

Create a Leads campaign for website conversions

Start by telling Meta the actual outcome: leads on your landing page, not instant-form curiosity.
  1. Create a new campaign and choose Leads.
  2. At the ad set level, choose Website rather than instant forms when lead quality matters.
  3. Use maximize number of conversions at the beginning.
  4. Select the correct pixel and Lead conversion event.
  5. Avoid setting a low cost-per-result goal too early; it can stop delivery before Meta has room to learn.
  6. Schedule the campaign for the next day at midnight so the daily budget is spread across a full day.
Timing detail Launching a daily-budget campaign at 7 PM can force Meta to spend the day's budget in a few hours, often creating high CPL. Start clean at the beginning of the day.
2

Target location, age, and broad interests with intent

The goal is not to find tiny perfect pockets. The goal is broad enough scale with enough relevance.

Use people living in this location when you need residents, not travelers. For a large country like India, filter by city size or handpick top metros depending on buying power and offer type.

Keep interests broad enough to avoid extreme CPM. For a digital agency course, examples include digital marketing, online marketing, lead generation, email marketing, internet marketing, and search engine marketing.

3

Research interests before you build ad sets

Do not stack interests forever inside one ad set, then wonder which one worked.

Use stacked seed interests only for research and suggestions. Once you find ideas, record them in a sheet and test them as separate ad sets whenever possible.

Dashboard suggestions — stack a few relevant seeds, then use suggested interests.
External interest tools — useful for generating ideas, but verify audience size inside Meta.
Graph API Explorer — advanced way to discover interests by query, then validate them in Ads Manager.
Profile signals — if you know a target buyer, inspect their visible interests, likes, books, tools, influencers, and brands.
Testing logic If digital marketing, affiliate marketing, and email marketing are all inside one ad set, you cannot tell which one produced low-quality or high-quality leads.
4

At the ad level, keep control of the message

Meta has many optimization toggles. Use them intentionally, not blindly.
Select the correct Facebook page and Instagram account.
Upload the image or video and review how it appears across placements.
Turn off multi-advertiser expansion at the start if you want cleaner testing.
Write primary text, headline, optional description, and choose the CTA button.
Use a clean display URL so the ad looks trustworthy.
Confirm the website event is tied to the correct pixel.

The transcript's default CTA choice is often Learn More. It has lower resistance than Sign Up or Buy Now when the landing page needs to explain before asking for action.

5

Custom and lookalike audiences compound over time

Your best future targeting often comes from the data you are collecting today.

A custom audience can come from a customer list, an engaged email segment, website visitors, lead events, page engagement, or people who watched videos. Meta hashes uploaded data, so partners can use the audience without seeing the raw list.

For uploads, quality beats volume. A list of 1,000 engaged subscribers or buyers can be more useful than 10,000 cold leads who never open emails. Add value fields when you can so Meta learns which customers matter most.

MODULE 06 AWARENESS · AIDA · CREATIVE TESTING COPY + DESIGN
1

Write for the audience's awareness level

Different ad sets contain different people. They should not all hear the same message.

The transcript uses five awareness levels: unaware, problem aware, solution aware, product aware, and most aware. A beginner audience may need the problem named for them. A comparison-shopper may respond to discounts, urgency, or a direct reason to choose you.

That is why the same product can need several angles. Email marketers, agency owners, freshers, and web developers may all be relevant, but each group needs a hook that sounds like it was written for them.

2

The first line sells the rest of the ad

In the feed, the first line is often all people see before "see more". Spend your best thinking there.

Use Ads Library research before writing. Search broad keywords in your niche and look for ads that have been running for weeks. Long-running ads are not proof, but they are strong clues that the advertiser is getting value.

Hook Rule
The ad does not sell the product.
It sells the click.

The landing page does the deeper selling. The ad should create enough relevance, curiosity, and trust that the right person wants to learn more.

Use a specific problem: "Is your cost per lead rising every month?"
Call out the audience directly: "Freshers and aspiring web developers..."
Use numbers and specificity: "Helped 173 people..." feels more real than "helped many".
Use open loops ethically: give enough information to create curiosity, then pay it off on the page.
3

Use AIDA until you earn the right to improvise

Attention, Interest, Desire, Action is simple because it matches the reader's path.
StageWhat it doesHow to write it
AttentionStops the scroll.One killer hook in roughly the first 125 characters.
InterestJoins the conversation already in their head.Name the pain, symptom, failed attempt, or situation.
DesireShows the better future.Explain how the solution makes life better, not just its features.

4

1024×1024Square format that can work widely, though dedicated verticals perform better in vertical inventory.

  • Keep text readable on mobile.
  • Add a button-like visual CTA where appropriate.
  • Use two to three colors and clean sans-serif fonts.
  • Use proof elements like screenshots, ratings, or served-counts.
Avoid
  • Clutter, tiny text, and too many fonts.
  • White and Facebook-blue dominant designs that blend into the UI.
  • Repeating the exact same message in headline, description, and image.
  • Videos longer than needed; under two minutes keeps more placements open.
5

Variation must be meaningful

Tiny design tweaks usually produce tiny learning. Test different angles, formats, and behaviors.

Quantity and quality both matter. Test image ads and video ads aggressively, but do not put three videos and one image in the same ad set at the beginning because the video can dominate spend before the image has a fair shot.

Use different creative types to reach different people inside the same broad interest: professional studio videos, authentic selfie videos, UGC-style testimonials, asymmetric designs, angle-based images, and native-looking text posts.

Understand the numbers that create CPL.

Cost per lead is not one thing. It is the result of CPM, CTR, CPC, landing page load quality, and landing page conversion. Fix the upstream number, and the downstream cost changes.

MODULE 07 CPM · CTR · CPC · FREQUENCY · CVR PERFORMANCE MATH
1

Let a campaign earn enough data before judging it

Early numbers are noisy. The transcript suggests three to seven days or around 10,000 impressions.

Do not panic because an ad set spent ₹20 and looks terrible. It has not had a fair test. Campaigns need enough impressions and enough budget spread across ad sets before their metrics mean anything.

Also remember: bad historical data can influence future learning. If you keep running poorly optimized campaigns, the account and pixel can develop weak benchmarks. Every campaign should be launched with clean tracking and a real optimization target.

2

CPM is the root cost

Meta charges for attention. Most other metrics are derived from how efficiently that attention becomes action.
Audience size — broader audiences usually reduce CPM; too narrow makes scale expensive.
Niche competition — more advertisers bidding for the same attention means higher CPM.
Campaign structure — one campaign, one ad set, one ad gives Meta little room to optimize.
Objective — awareness is cheaper than leads; sales can be more expensive because the target behavior is rarer.
Tracking quality — bad pixel or event setup starves Meta of feedback.
Seasonality — Diwali, Black Friday, and sale periods lift competition across industries.
Quality rankings — below-average quality, engagement, or conversion rankings lift cost.
3

CTR and CPC are copy/creative report cards

If CPM is what attention costs, CTR is how well your ad uses it.

The transcript uses roughly 0.5% to 1% CTR as a reasonable starting range, with around 1% as a healthy target in many cases. Higher is not always better if the ad attracts curious, low-quality clicks.

CPC formula If CPM is low and CTR is high, cost per link click falls naturally. You do not optimize CPC directly as much as you optimize CPM and CTR.

CTR improves through hooks, clear CTAs, native-looking creative, good design, action language, relevant awareness level, clean display URL, and a CTA button that matches intent: Learn More, Watch More, Book Now, Sign Up, and so on.

4

Link click to landing page view exposes leakage

People can click the ad and still never load the page. That gap burns budget quietly.

Calculate it manually: landing page views ÷ link clicks. If 100 people click and only 80 load the page, your ratio is 80%.

Improve speed and hosting reliability.
Check Core Web Vitals: LCP under 2.5s, FID under 100ms, CLS under 0.1.
Fix SSL, 404s, broken redirects, and downtime monitoring.
Make sure the ad is attracting the right audience so people do not bounce before the page loads.
5

Landing page conversion is your responsibility

If people reach the page and do not convert, the answer is usually on the page, not inside Ads Manager.

Landing page conversion is improved by strong copy, aligned expectations, fast loading, social proof, screenshots of testimonials, multimedia, clear offer, clean UI, mobile-first design, and no distracting navigation.

Use A/B tests for landing pages. Even a headline change can move the final CPL because every downstream conversion rate compounds the economics.

1

Use UTMs when revenue happens outside Meta

Meta can show lead cost, but you need to know which ad set created customers.

UTM parameters are small codes added to the URL. The transcript focuses on three fields:

utm_sourceWhere the visitor came from: Facebook ads, Google ads, organic, affiliate, newsletter.
utm_campaignIn this workflow, use the ad set name, such as digital marketing or online marketing.

Core Idea
Low CPL is not proof.
Revenue by ad set is proof.

An ad set can generate cheap leads that never buy. UTMs let you trace the final sale back to source, ad set, and ad creative.

2

Hidden fields capture the URL values

The lead never types these values. The form pulls them from the URL and saves them silently.

In Elementor Pro, add hidden fields for source, campaign, and content. Under advanced settings, use a dynamic request parameter with GET and set the parameter names to utm_source, utm_campaign, and utm_content.

Send every form submission to a sheet

The WordPress backend is not a performance dashboard. Push the data somewhere you can calculate.

  3. Map name, email, phone, source, campaign, and content into Google Sheets.
  4. Add a date/time column; the transcript uses a Pabbly local human-now shortcode.

MODULE 09 CAC · TESTING · BREAKDOWNS · BIDDING OPTIMIZATION SYSTEM
1

Know your maximum CAC before touching optimizations

If you do not know what you can afford to pay for a customer, you do not know what "good" means.

Start from the business model. For a course launch, calculate expected students, price, GST/tax, ad spend, ad GST, operations cost, and net revenue. If 50 students from ₹1,00,000 ad spend keeps the launch profitable, CAC is ₹2,000.

For webinars, work backwards: required customers → webinar attendees → registrations → leads per day → daily budget. For ecommerce, include product cost, shipping, platform cost, selling price, and the CAC ceiling that still leaves profit.

Optimization Target
CPL is useful only after CAC is clear.

A lead cost can look expensive but still be profitable if sales conversion is strong. Or it can look cheap and destroy the business if no one buys.

2

Build projections so decisions are not emotional

The sheet tells you when to increase budget, fix show-up rate, improve conversion, or stop.

For launch funnels, break the promotion into webinar windows instead of waiting a month for one event. Revenue from early webinars can fund the next week's ads. If projections slip, you know what lever to pull: WhatsApp reminders, landing page copy, show-up rate, close rate, or budget.

3

Test creative first, then copy, then dynamic creative

Creative differences are usually bigger than copy differences, so test the visual pattern first.
  1. Test different creative types: text image, video, reel, slideshow, UGC, proof image.
  2. Once a creative type wins, test two or three copy variations against that winning creative.
  3. When winning combinations are known, use Dynamic Creative to let Meta combine selected images, texts, headlines, and descriptions.
  4. Do not use Dynamic Creative before UTM and revenue testing teach you which combinations actually create customers.
Dynamic creative caveat It can tell you the overall dynamic ad works, but it will not always give a clean revenue answer for every possible combination.
4

Optimize audiences through size, indirect interests, and breakdowns

After basics work, expand intelligently instead of randomly adding interests.
Audience size — try to shape interest audiences into roughly 3-5 million where possible.
Indirect interests — real estate buyers may also be investors, stock-market followers, or wealth-building readers.
Naked targeting — use age/location without interests inside the same campaign after other ad sets have trained the campaign.
Lookalikes — create from leads, buyers, thank-you page visitors, engaged email lists, or website events.
Retargeting — target landing page visitors who did not reach the thank-you page.
Breakdowns — inspect age, gender, region, platform, placement, device, and destination.

When making major changes, duplicate the ad set, edit the duplicate, and keep the old one available. If the new version fails, you can return to the previous working version.

5

Bid strategies are advanced controls, not first moves

Use them after metrics, tracking, creative, and audience testing have been cleaned up.
StrategyMeaningRisk
Highest volume / lowest costSpend the budget to get as many conversions as possible.Costs can float higher if the system finds volume.
Cost per result goal / cost capAverage around a target CPL or CPA over time.Volume may drop if the target is too strict.
Bid capDo not bid above a strict ceiling.Ads may barely deliver if the market requires higher bids.
ROAS goalOptimize for minimum return when purchase value is passed back.Requires enough reliable purchase-value data.

  • Test creative first, copy second, dynamic creative third.
  • Use breakdowns to split winning demographics, placements, and devices.
  • Use cost caps, bid caps, and ROAS goals only after the account has data.

FB Ads Mastery · Module 10

Automated rules for controlled scaling.

When budgets get large, you cannot watch Ads Manager every minute. Automated rules let Meta notify you, pause losers, increase winners, and reduce overlap before small problems become expensive.

MODULE 10 AUTOMATED RULES · OVERLAP · BUDGET GUARDS SCALING PART 1
1

Rules are trigger-action instructions

If a condition becomes true, Meta takes the action or alerts you.

A rule can pause an ad set when CPL goes above your cap, increase budget by 20% when results stay strong, decrease budget when efficiency drops, or simply send a notification so a human can decide.

Use notifications when unsure You do not have to let Meta make every decision. A notification-only rule can surface issues without changing the account automatically.
2

Reduce auction overlap and audience fragmentation

At scale, your own ad sets can start competing with each other.
Auction overlapYour campaigns or ad sets enter the same auction and bid against each other.
Audience fragmentationSeveral ad sets contain overlapping audiences that Meta is forced to treat separately.
Budget reallocationMeta can turn off overlapping ad sets and move budget to the remaining stronger set.
Combined audienceMeta can combine fragmented interests into one larger ad set when scaling.

The transcript does not recommend combining audiences too early. Test ad sets separately first so you know cost and quality. Once several audiences work and overlap becomes a problem, rules can help consolidate them.

3

Custom rules are where the real control lives

Prebuilt rules are helpful, but custom rules let you define the exact metric and action.
Rule ideaExample conditionPossible action
Cut losersCost per result > ₹50 and results > 20.Turn off ad set or send notification.
Scale winnersCost per result < ₹30 and results > 100 in last 7 days.Increase daily budget by 20% with a max cap.
Protect frequencyFrequency > 2 or 3 depending on campaign type.Pause ad or notify creative team.
Control spendBudget reaches a defined maximum.Stop increasing at that cap.

Always combine quality and quantity conditions. For example, do not increase budget just because CPL is low if the ad set only generated two leads. Give Meta enough evidence before the rule acts.

4

Rules behave differently at each level

Campaign, ad set, and ad level controls are not interchangeable.
Campaign level — turn campaign on/off or notify based on broad spend, CPA, impressions, or other conditions.
Ad set level — most useful for scaling because you can increase/decrease daily budget by percent or fixed amount.
Ad level — mainly turn individual ads on or off based on performance.
Manual bid automation — after bid caps are in use, rules can raise or lower the bid by a fixed value.
5

Rules are not a replacement for the funnel

They protect scaling, but they cannot fix weak sales, weak nurture, or weak conversion.

At low budgets, you may not need advanced automation. At large budgets, a few hours of inefficient spend can be expensive. That is when rules become a serious performance-marketer tool.

Module 10 automation checklist
  • Create notification-only rules first if you want human review.
  • Use automated rules to pause high-CPL ad sets, protect frequency, and scale proven winners.
  • Set maximum daily budget caps when allowing automated increases.
  • Run rules on useful time windows, such as last 7 days, instead of reacting to tiny samples.
  • Use rules for scaling, not for replacing judgment.

Scale without pretending costs stay flat.

Scaling means spending more while keeping CPA acceptable. It does not mean doubling budget and expecting double leads at the same price.

MODULE 11 VERTICAL · HORIZONTAL · COMBINED SCALING SCALING PART 2
1

Scaling means more spend at tolerable CPA

The first audience pocket is usually the warmest. More spend reaches colder people.

When a campaign starts, Meta often finds the people most likely to convert first. When you add budget, it must reach beyond that first responsive pocket. CTR can fall, conversion rate can fall, frequency can rise, and CPL can climb.

Scaling Reality
More budget does not guarantee more leads.
Sometimes it buys the same leads at higher cost.

That is why scaling is gradual, measured against your upper cap, and supported by fresh creative and new audiences.

2

Vertical scaling: go deeper into the same winner

If frequency is still under 2 and quality is strong, start by adding budget to the existing winner.
Daily budget levelSuggested increaseStabilization wait
Under ₹2,500/dayUp to 50%Wait 5-7 days before the next increase.
₹2,500-₹5,000/dayUp to 30%Wait 5-7 days.
Above ₹5,000/day10-20%Move slowly to avoid disrupting learning.

Vertical scaling can also include expanding age range, adding locations, using multi-advertiser expansion, or reaching beyond a custom audience. While doing this, refresh ads because higher spend fatigues creatives faster.

3

Horizontal scaling: find new people

When frequency rises beyond 3, you need more audience, not just more money.
New interests — use persona research: influencers, books, magazines, websites, tools, brands, and behaviors.
New hooks — a marketing-book audience needs a different hook from a digital-marketing-interest audience.
Open targeting — add an ad set with no interests inside the same trained campaign.
Lookalikes — build from email lists, thank-you page visitors, leads, buyers, video viewers, or engagement audiences.
Retargeting — keep page visitors who did not convert in a separate hot-audience campaign.
Open targeting placement Put open targeting inside the same campaign that already has working ad sets. The transcript's hypothesis is that the campaign history helps teach the broad ad set who to find.
4

Combined scaling: merge winners, then use CBO

After vertical and horizontal testing, combine proven pockets into larger, cleaner audiences.

If online marketing 20-30 and online marketing 30-40 both work, create a new combined ad set for online marketing 20-40. If digital marketing and online marketing both work, test combining them into a larger audience.

Once several combined ad sets stabilize, move from ad set budget optimization to campaign budget optimization, also called Advantage campaign budget in newer Meta language. This lets Meta decide which winning ad set should receive more budget each day.

5

Know when the campaign has reached its ceiling

Stopping is a skill. Past a point, a campaign can spend more without producing meaningfully more leads.

The module's example shows CPL rising as budget scales. When CPL crosses the upper cap and lead volume stops increasing meaningfully, pause vertical scaling, stabilize, test new creatives and angles, then try horizontal or combined scaling. If that ceiling remains, build new campaigns, offers, or lead magnets instead of forcing the old one.

Module 11 scaling checklist
  • Use automated rules before heavy scaling.
  • Scale vertically first when frequency is under 2.
  • Use 50%, 30%, or 10-20% budget increases based on daily budget size.
  • Do not test and scale at the same time.
  • Use horizontal scaling when frequency rises and audience is exhausted.
  • Combine winners and move to CBO only after enough proof.

FB Ads Mastery · Module 12

Build a Facebook funnel that warms up the market.

When cold lead campaigns hit a ceiling, use Facebook itself as a nurture platform: value first, proof second, offer third.

MODULE 12 TOF · MOF · BOF · HOT AUDIENCES FULL-FUNNEL STRATEGY
1

Do not be salesy in every impression

A cold audience that has only seen pitches becomes resistant. Value content lowers that resistance.

This is not needed for every small account. It becomes useful for personal brands, larger advertisers, and brands that need reach beyond the warm slice already responding to lead ads.

The idea is simple: spend a small amount showing useful, non-sales content; then show proof; then retarget engaged people with lead generation or sales ads. The final CPL may look similar, but the audience has had multiple trust-building touches before the ask.

Influence plus lead If you spend ₹20 warming someone and ₹30 converting them, the total may still be ₹50, but you also created brand recall and trust.
2

Understand attribution before judging funnels

Facebook gives credit based on rules and windows, not perfect truth.

Attribution is how Meta decides which ad gets credit for a conversion. The transcript explains last-touch attribution, one-day click, seven-day click, one-day view, and seven-day click plus one-day view windows.

For time-sensitive campaigns, shorter windows can help you read faster. For normal campaigns, broader click/view windows can capture more delayed behavior, though modeled data and platform changes can affect accuracy. Treat attribution as a directional tool, not a perfect accounting system.

3

Top of funnel: attract with value, not a CTA

Use low-cost objectives to reach cold audiences with content they can consume inside the feed.
Brand awarenessBuild recall cheaply with non-sales posts.
EngagementPromote posts, carousels, memes, quotes, tips, or simple value images.
Video viewsWarm people through useful short videos or tool tutorials.
Landing page viewsSend cold people to broad, useful blog posts.

Use interests that already worked in lead generation. Do not start brand-awareness funnels before you know which audiences can eventually produce good leads.

4

Middle and bottom of funnel: proof, then offer

Top funnel attracts. Middle funnel builds trust. Bottom funnel asks for the conversion.
StageContentGoal
TopHow-to posts, tips, memes, broad blog posts, simple value images, short videos.Attract cold people and create first engagement.
MiddleCustomer testimonials, media mentions, podcast clips, meetups, corporate trainings, authority photos.Build trust and credibility with engaged people.
BottomLead magnet, VSL, webinar, sales page, booking campaign, purchase campaign.Convert the warmest recent audience.

Middle-of-funnel proof can be simple: a photo from a conference, a media mention, a customer result, a podcast clip, or a corporate training snippet. It should feel like value or credibility, not a constant sales pitch.

5

Build Hot 28 and Hot 7 style audiences

Group recent engagers so the next layer targets people who already showed attention.
Hot 28 — people who interacted with top-of-funnel content in the last 28 days.
Hot 7 — people with very recent bottom-funnel intent in the last 7 days.
Website actions — visited specific pages, spent more time on site, visited blog posts.
Video actions — completed or watched at least 15 seconds of selected top-funnel videos.
Instagram actions — engaged, followed, visited profile, sent DM, or engaged with an ad.
Facebook actions — page likes, post engagement, ad engagement, event engagement.

Create the component audiences first, then combine them in saved audiences for middle and bottom campaigns. The more you spend at the top, the more the Hot 28 pool refreshes automatically.

Module 12 funnel checklist
  • Prove winning interests with lead generation before using them for top-funnel branding.
  • Use low-cost objectives to warm large cold pools with useful content.
  • Combine top-funnel engagers into a Hot 28-style audience.
  • Show trust content to Hot 28, then use Hot 7-style audiences for lead or sales campaigns.
  • Track total influence and eventual conversion, not only immediate lead cost.
