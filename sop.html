STEP 3
Niche Neighbor Analysis
Find 3-5 channels that serve the same ready-to-buy audience but don't compete directly.
▶ YouTube🔍 VidIQ✦ ChatGPT
ℹ️
Why this matters: YouTube cross-recommends videos from similar-topic channels. If your topics align with a niche neighbor's best-performing content, YouTube will test recommending your client's content to their audience. This is free exposure to an already-qualified audience.
  • Identify where the ready-to-buy viewer already gets content on YouTube
  • Find 3-5 complementary channels (NOT direct competitors — no channel with identical offer)
  • For each channel: extract top 10 performing AND 5 most recent videos
  • Record: title patterns, thumbnail styles, video length, comment themes
  • Identify the gap — what topics are they NOT covering that your client can own?
  • Note: which videos have more views than the channel's subscriber count (proven marketing package)

Phase 04 · Days 12–14
Playlist Funnel Builder
The 5-video funnel is the core conversion machine. Each video is a domino that knocks over the next. This is what makes YouTube a sales system — not just a content platform.
ℹ️
Critical Distinction: Trena's course is designed for course creators selling $97-$497 products. Our clients sell high-ticket coaching ($3K-$20K+). The funnel structure is the same — but the CTA goes to a calendar booking page, not a purchase page. Trust-building is more critical. Video 5 sets up a call — not a checkout.
The 5-Video Funnel Structure
1
Awareness — The "Why" Video
Goal: Make them aware of the core problem your client's offer solves. Attract the right viewer. Make them think "this person understands exactly what I'm going through." Open a loop that makes them need Video 2.
Keyword type: Problem-focused (Awareness keywords)
CTA: Drive to Video 2 — tell them exactly which video and why they need it
Example: "Why Your Discovery Calls Aren't Converting to Clients (And It's Not Your Close)"
2
Education — The Quick Win
Goal: One actionable tip that delivers a quick win and builds trust. Prove competence. Make them think "I can actually do this."
Keyword type: Solution-focused (Consideration keywords)
CTA: Soft CTA to offer woven naturally + drive to Video 3
Example: "The 3-Question Framework That Pre-Qualifies Prospects Before the Call"
3
Reframe — The Myth Buster
Goal: Challenge the limiting belief that's preventing them from taking action. Remove the objection they've been using as an excuse.
Keyword type: Misconception-focused (Consideration keywords)
CTA: Soft CTA + drive to Video 4
Example: "The Myth That More Leads = More Clients (What Actually Fixes Your Pipeline)"
4
Insights — The Advanced Strategy
Goal: Deeper specificity. Position the client as the go-to expert. This is where credibility compounds. The viewer starts thinking about working with the coach.
Keyword type: Advanced/strategy-focused (Consideration keywords)
CTA: Soft CTA + drive to Video 5 with explicit reason why they need it
Example: "How My Clients Book 10+ Qualified Calls/Month Without Paid Ads"
5
CTA — The Offer Video
Goal: Introduce the offer as the next logical step. 60-90 second hard pitch. Specific results, guarantee mention, clear next step = book a call.
Keyword type: Decision/offer-focused (Decision keywords)
CTA: HARD CTA ONLY HERE. Full offer introduction with benefits, results, guarantee, booking link
Example: "Want a Predictable Flow of Qualified Sales Calls? Here's How We Build It"
CTA Architecture Rules (High-Ticket Version)
Soft CTA (Videos 1-4)
"By the way, if you want to skip the DIY phase entirely and have this built for you — there's a link in the description below."
Weave in organically. Never feel salesy. One per video maximum.
Hard CTA (Video 5 ONLY)
"If you want a system that brings you [specific result], I have [X] spots open this month — book a free strategy call at the link in the description."
60-90 seconds. Include results, guarantee, urgency, exact next step.
Banned CTA Phrases — Never Use These
🚫
Never end a video with: "In summary..." / "To recap..." / "To wrap up..." / "Just to recap what we covered..." — These signal the video is over. The viewer should feel like the video ended abruptly and WANT to keep watching. Drive them to the next video instead.
Forward-Momentum CTA Templates (Videos 1-4)
"Now if you [belief/realization from this video], you're going to really want to watch the next video where I show you [specific thing they'll learn]. I'll see you there."

Example: "Now if you believe your pipeline problem is a lead quality issue and not a volume issue, you're going to really want to watch the next video where I show you the exact 3-question pre-qualification framework. I'll see you there."

THUMBNAIL
Thumbnail Creation System
◆ Canva▶ YouTube Research
Click-Worthy Thumbnail Formula
What Makes a Thumbnail Work
  • Coach always in the thumbnail — faces connect emotionally
  • 2-4 words MAX — large, bold, readable at small size
  • Emotion + curiosity = clicks (shock, frustration, excitement)
  • Title and thumbnail complement WITHOUT repeating
  • High contrast — stands out against YouTube's white/grey background
  • Show don't tell — before/after, numbers, visual proof
What Kills a Thumbnail
  • Too much text — more than 4 words is unreadable at small size
  • No emotion — neutral face blends into the page
  • No contrast — dark text on dark background, light on light
  • Thumbnail repeats title word-for-word (no extra information)
  • No face — objects and graphics underperform faces
  • Clutter — too many elements competing for attention
Title + Thumbnail Alignment Rule
The thumbnail adds mystery to the title — it does NOT repeat it.

Title: "You're Wasting Time Doing THIS..."
Thumbnail text: "STOP THIS NOW" + big X over a calendar

The viewer sees the thumbnail, reads the title, and the two together create a curiosity gap that makes clicking feel like the only logical thing to do.
A/B TEST
A/B Testing Protocol
▶ YouTube Studio
When to Test
  • CTR below 4% after 48-72 hours
  • Title doesn't create curiosity or urgency
  • Thumbnail isn't emotionally engaging
  • Video not ranking for targeted keyword
Testing Rules
  • Change ONE thing at a time — title OR thumbnail, never both
  • Track for 7 days after change
  • Use YouTube's native A/B test tool (up to 3 thumbnails at once)
  • If CTR increases → keep new version permanently

Phase 07 · Client Executes
Film & Edit
The client films. Your team edits. The Video Game Plan is the brief. This tab gives the client everything they need to film right the first time — and gives your editor the standards they need to deliver.
FILMING
Client Filming Instructions
Send this to the client before every filming session
Minimum Viable Setup
  • Camera: iPhone 12+ or any Android with 1080p — tripod mounted, not handheld
  • Audio: Lapel mic (DJI Mic or Rode Wireless Go) OR sit close to camera
  • Lighting: Ring light OR sit facing a window (face lit, no shadows)
  • Background: Clean, professional or intentionally designed — no clutter
  • Frame: Eyes at upper third, some headroom, camera at eye level
Filming Sequence (Order Matters)
  • 1. Film A-Roll first — main talking head, full video
  • 2. Take thumbnail photos — BEFORE changing outfit. Multiple expressions.
  • 3. Capture B-Roll — screen recordings, demos, supporting visuals
  • Record in 1080p minimum, 4K preferred
  • Film in natural speaking style — Video Game Plan is a guide, not a script to memorize
⚠️
Thumbnail photo reminder: This is the most common mistake. Clients change outfits after filming A-Roll and forget thumbnail photos. The thumbnail must match the video. Set a reminder in the filming checklist: "STOP — take thumbnail photos before changing."
EDITING
Editing Standards
Non-negotiable quality standards for every video
  • Remove all filler words and dead air — "um", "uh", long pauses, false starts
  • Pacing: Slightly faster than comfortable — YouTube viewers tolerate information density, not dead air
  • Audio: Consistent volume throughout. No background hum. Normalize audio levels.
  • Text overlays: Add at key points — stats, frameworks, key phrases. Reinforce spoken words.
  • B-roll cuts: Cut to B-roll or screen recording when coach explains something visual
  • Chapter markers: Add for SEO and watch time (timestamps in description)
  • Opening: No intro animation, no "hey guys welcome back", no channel intro — hook starts in second 1
  • Ending: Abrupt cut after CTA — no outro music that plays over silence, no "thanks for watching"
🚫
Banned editing patterns: Long intro animations · "Welcome back to my channel" openers · "Thanks for watching, see you next time" endings · Slow fades to black · Recapping what was just covered ("So to summarize what we just learned..."). These tank retention.
